The rules.

Technology alone won't fix this. What forces anyone to look is law. Rules are moving from "please report" to "you must check, and your goods can be stopped at the border". But each rule reaches a different part of this case.

Five levels of pressure

Level 1 · voluntary

Guidelines

UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (2011); OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises (updated 2023)
Level 2 · disclose

Reporting

EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD, in force 2023)
Level 3 · duty of care

Due diligence

German Supply Chain Act (2023); EU CSDDD (in force 2024, later delayed and narrowed by the 2025 "Omnibus" package; check current status)
Level 4 · border

Import bans

US Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (2022); EU Forced Labour Regulation (applies from December 2027)
Level 5 · product

Product data

EU Ecodesign Regulation (2024) and Digital Product Passports, due for textiles and footwear

The lesson

What this says about system change

Supply-chain lawsThese reach the brands buying Albanian shoes and chrome, but not the resort investors, who sell no goods into Europe.
AccessionEU membership talks reach the Albanian government itself, which is why the resort was paused only after Brussels and protesters both pushed.
Public pressureThe protests did what no rule had done: work at Zvërnec stopped in June 2026. Voluntary, mandatory and grassroots pressure work together.
