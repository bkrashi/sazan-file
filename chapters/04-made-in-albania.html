Made in Albania

Who mines, sews and profits from what Albania exports?

Beyond the coast, Albania's economy runs on what it sells: chrome ore for the world's stainless steel, and shoes and clothes sewn for European brands. The same questions of ownership, wages and evidence apply. But the supply chains are separate from the resort projects, and nothing on record connects them.

In this chapter
  • Chrome ore is Albania's top export in the customs records reviewed, much of it shipped by offshore traders.
  • Inditex companies and Decathlon appear as buyers of Albanian shoes and T-shirts (2020–2022 records).
  • The legal minimum is €517 a month; underpayment was documented in 2016.
  • Follow three products from raw material to the shop, and see what can be verified at each stage.

Two industries

Chrome and fason

Chrome · Bulqizë

Ore for stainless steel

AlbChrome, registered in 2000, mines at Bulqizë; its recorded buyers include Eti Krom (Turkey), Mitsubishi Corp. RtM Japan and Marubeni Tetsugen. Traders in Hong Kong and the UAE ship most Albanian chrome ore in the records.

An independent miners' union was recognised in 2019 and says nine members were dismissed; the company disputes this. Allegation Miners boycotted work in March 2025.

Garments & shoes · fason

Sewn for European brands

workshops cut and stitch to order. Customs records show ITX Turkey, Massimo Dutti México, Stradivarius México and Decathlon Indonesia among buyers, mostly 2020–2022.

For buyers

What a buyer can verify today, and what it can't

Can verify
  • Which exporter shipped the goods, and when (customs records)
  • Who is declared as owner of an Albanian supplier (registers)
  • The legal minimum wage that should be paid
Can't verify yet
  • Which workshop sewed a given shoe or T-shirt
  • Which mine a given cargo of chrome came from, after traders
  • What workers are actually paid today; no factory-level data newer than 2016 was found
